An Insight into Freemasonry

A Moral Code. A Study in the Art of Life.

Our goal is to provide a positive insight into what Freemasonry has to offer. Within these pages you will find out about Freemasonry in general, about Cranleigh Freemasons and the Province of Surrey.

Freemasonry may be summed up in the following manner: a moral code; a study in the art of life; a way of life.

The Fraternity is Based on Three Principles

What We Stand For

I

Brotherly Love

Every true Freemason will show tolerance and respect for the opinions of others and behave with kindness and understanding to his fellow man.

II

Relief

Freemasons are taught to practise charity, and to care not only for their own, but also for the community as a whole, both by charitable giving and voluntary efforts and works.

III

Truth

Freemasons strive for truth, requiring high moral standards and aiming to achieve them in their own lives.

"The purpose of Freemasonry is the self development of the individual as an independent, thinking, ethical, compassionate man of integrity. It is often said 'Freemasonry takes good men and makes them better.' That is true — it also gives us great friendships with people we know we can trust."

About

Freemasonry

An Approach to Life

What is Freemasonry?

Freemasonry teaches moral lessons and self-knowledge through participation in a progression of allegorical two-part plays, which are learnt by heart and performed within each Lodge.

Freemasonry offers its members an approach to life which seeks to reinforce thoughtfulness for others, kindness in the community, honesty in business, courtesy in society and fairness in all things. Members are urged to regard the interests of the family as paramount but importantly Freemasonry also teaches and practices concern for people, care for the less fortunate and help for those in need.

Why do people join and remain members?

People become Freemasons for a variety of reasons, some as the result of family tradition, others upon the introduction of a friend or out of a curiosity to know what it is all about.

Those who become active members and who grow in Freemasonry do so principally because they enjoy it. They enjoy the challenges and fellowship that Freemasonry offers. Participation in the dramatic presentation of moral lessons and in the working of a Lodge provides a member with a unique opportunity to learn more about himself and encourages him to live in such a way that he will always be in search of becoming a better man.

Each Freemason is required to learn and show humility through initiation. Then, by progression through a series of degrees he gains insight into increasingly complex moral and philosophical concepts, and accepts a variety of challenges and responsibilities which are both stimulating and rewarding.

What promises do Freemasons take?

New members make solemn promises concerning their conduct in the Lodge and society. These promises are similar to those taken in court or upon entering the Armed Services or many other organisations. Each member also promises to keep confidential the traditional methods of proving he is a Freemason which he would use when visiting a Lodge where he is not known.

The much publicised 'traditional penalties' for failure to observe these undertakings were removed from the promises in 1986. They were always symbolic, not literal, and refer only to the pain any decent man should feel at the thought of violating his word. Members also undertake to not make use of their membership for personal gain or advancement.

Is Freemasonry a religion?

Freemasonry is not a religion. It has no theology and does not teach any route to salvation. A belief in God, however, is an essential requirement for membership and Freemasonry encourages its members to be active in their own religions as well as in society at large.

Although every Lodge meeting is opened and closed with a prayer and its ceremonies reflect the essential truths and moral teachings common to many of the world's great religions, no discussion of religion is permitted in Lodge meetings.

Who can join?

Membership is open to men of all faiths who are law-abiding, of good character and who acknowledge a belief in God. Freemasonry is a multi-racial and multi-cultural organisation. It has attracted men of goodwill from all sectors of the community into membership.

Is Freemasonry a secret society?

Freemasonry is not a secret society, but Lodge meetings, like meetings of many other social and professional associations, are private occasions open only to members. Freemasons are encouraged to speak openly about their membership, while remembering that they undertake not to use it for their own or anyone else's advancement.

The rules and aims of Freemasonry are available to the public. The Masonic Year Book contains the names of all national office-holders and lists of all Lodges with details of their meeting dates and places. Freemason's Hall in London is open to the public and 'open days' are held in many provincial centres.

Is Freemasonry involved in politics?

Freemasonry is definitely not a political organisation, it has no political agenda, and discussion of politics is not permitted at Lodge meetings.

Is Freemasonry involved in the community?

From its earliest days, Freemasonry has been involved in charitable activities, and since its inception it has provided support for many widows and orphans of Freemasons as well as for others within the community. All monies raised for charity are drawn from amongst Freemasons, their families and friends, while grants and donations are made to Masonic and non-Masonic charities alike.

Over the past five years alone Freemasonry has raised more than £75m for a wide range of charitable purposes including those involved in medical research, community care, education and work with young people.

Who We Are

A Brotherhood in the Heart of Surrey

Cranleigh Lodge No.3445 is a Masonic lodge situated in the heart of Surrey, working under the United Grand Lodge of England and the Provincial Grand Lodge of Surrey.[cite: 1]

Our motto, Festina Lente — 'Make Haste Slowly' — reflects a philosophy of measured progress, integrity, and enduring brotherhood.[cite: 1]

Lodge No. 3445

Heritage

A Brief History of the Cranleigh Lodge No 3445

Currently the Cranleigh Lodge has some 48 members and celebrated it’s Centenary in November 2010.[cite: 1] The Lodge can look back on a very successful past; it has been and remains a significant force for good in the community.[cite: 1] It has supported many local organisations including Cranleigh Scouts, the Village Hospital, Citizens Advice, CHASE, GUTS, and more recently The Surrey/Sussex Air Ambulance.[cite: 1]

1910's

The history begins in 1910 when it was consecrated in the old Village Hall which was its first home.[cite: 1] The old Village Hall was situated on the Ewhurst Road and is not to be confused with the current Village Hall which was built in 1933.[cite: 1] A very distinguished Church of England clergyman helped to consecrate the Lodge: the Rev Canon Herbert William Turner M.A.[cite: 1] The Provincial Grand Master led the Dedication team, The Hon Sir Thomas Bucknill M.P.[cite: 1]

The first Worshipful Master was W. Bro. Frank Gustavus Naumann, a wealthy local merchant.[cite: 1] Two early tragedies beset the Lodge: the Secretary, Fred Hallows died in 1912 and on the 7th May 1915 the British transatlantic passenger liner Lusitania was torpedoed by a German submarine, taking the life of W. Bro. Frank Neumann.[cite: 1]

1950's

In 1953, the Lodge sold the Masonic Hall to the British Legion for £5000 but continued to rent the Hall for £100 per annum.[cite: 1] The Lodge and the Legion had reversed roles![cite: 1]

Local builder and devoted member Ernest Samuel Warren gave the Lodge the site the present Masonic Hall stands on today in Village Way and laid the foundation stone on 10th November 1954.[cite: 1] The new Masonic Hall was opened by the Provincial Grand Master, R.W. Bro. Lieut-Col. H. A. Mann O.B.E. M.C. on 12th October 1955.[cite: 1]

1990's - 2000's

In 1997, the Surrey Festival supported the Masonic Foundation for the Sick and Aged and it realized the sum of £7.5 million.[cite: 1] The Cranleigh Lodge’s contribution was £41,000, the 11th highest in Surrey.[cite: 1] Latterly for the 2008 Surrey Festival the Cranleigh Lodge contributed £11,000 towards a total of £3.6 million raised for the Royal Masonic Institute for Girls and Boys.[cite: 1]

In 1985 the Lodge decided to retire its existing Banner, gifted to it in 1911.[cite: 1] A new Banner was dedicated by the Deputy Provincial Grand Master W. Bro. P. A. Toler on 9th April 1986.[cite: 1]

The Lodge

Life as a Member

Membership of Cranleigh Lodge offers a unique framework for personal growth, friendship and community. Each meeting combines the formal ceremonial work of the Lodge with a convivial Festive Board — a social dinner where members and guests share fellowship.[cite: 1]

Ceremonial Work

Lodge meetings follow traditional Masonic ceremony, including the conferral of degrees and the performance of allegorical ritual that imparts moral lessons.[cite: 1]

The Festive Board

After every meeting, members and guests gather for the Festive Board — a formal dinner with toasts, laughter and conversation.[cite: 1]

Charitable Work

The lodge actively supports both Masonic and non-Masonic charities, making a real difference in the local community and beyond.[cite: 1]

Personal Development

From memorising ritual to taking on office, membership offers a continuous path of learning, responsibility and achievement.[cite: 1]
